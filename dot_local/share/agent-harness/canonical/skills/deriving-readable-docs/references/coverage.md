# Coverage (steps 8, 12 and 16 of SKILL.md)

Recall is: of the facts a newcomer must know, how many the guide states. Precision is the checkers' job (steps 10 and 13). This file adds the recall side. The lister makes one list of facts; every grade in the run uses that same list, so the figures compare. Keep each run's files in the work folder: `facts.txt`, `grades-<label>.md`, and what `coverage.py` writes (`coverage-<label>.json`, `coverage-<label>.todo.md`, `coverage.md`, one row for each label). A label already recorded is refused.

`<facts>` and `<grades>` below are `facts.txt` and `grades-<label>.md` in the work folder.

## Grade a guide (steps 8, 12 and 16)

1. Once per run, step 8 only: one fresh lister session (`Seat: lead (skill writer), Opus 5.5 medium`), briefed with `references/coverage-lister.md`, the repo path and the work folder. It sees the repo and not the guide. It writes `facts.txt`. `python3 -I <skill>/scripts/coverage.py --facts facts.txt <repo>` stops on a malformed row, a repeated id or a row whose cited line the repo lacks: send the file back to the lister before any grader starts.
2. One fresh grader session (`Seat: reviewer (skill checker), Opus 5.5 medium`) for each grade, briefed with `references/coverage-grader.md`, `facts.txt`, `GUIDE.md` and the repo. It writes `grades-<label>.md`. A grader is never the lister, and never a writer of the guide.
3. `python3 -I <skill>/scripts/coverage.py <facts> <grades> GUIDE.md <repo> --label <label> --work <work> --budget <the whole-guide budget of step 4>`. Labels: `first` (step 8), `repair` (step 12), `final` (step 16). It counts COVERED, PARTIAL and MISSING (a quote that is not in the guide turns the row into MISSING), routes each fact, fits the to-do to the room and writes it. `--budget` is required: the room is that budget less the words the guide has now, counted as `check_citations.py --max-words` counts them.

## The to-do (steps 8 and 12)

The to-do lists the PARTIAL and MISSING facts, ranked core first, then MISSING before PARTIAL, and stops where the word room ends. The room and its estimate are printed at the top: 25 words a fact (`--words-per-fact` changes it). The facts that do not fit go under `## Did not fit` in the to-do and in `coverage.md`; no writer works them, and the report says how many there were. A grade of the guide as it stands later (step 12) lists them again, if room is left.

- **A PARTIAL fact is an edit.** The to-do names the line to change (`edit line A4.3f9a in place`) and puts it under the section file that holds it. The writer opens that line, changes it so it says the fact (the value, unit, default, limit, failure result and the condition it lacked), and keeps its id. A PARTIAL fact never gets a second line: a duplicate is the failure this rule prevents. When the to-do says the routing is a guess, the writer finds the line that says the fact and edits it; it adds none.
- **A MISSING fact is a new line.** The to-do names the nearest line of its section that cites the same file (`add next to A4.3f9a`): the new line goes directly after it (a table row, in that row's table: a setting, a limit or a failure result goes in `### Defaults and limits` or `### When things fail`). It never goes at the top of the section. When no line cites the file, it goes after the section's last prose line.
- **Appendix writers.** One fresh writer for each section the to-do names (`Seat: lead (skill writer), Opus 5.5 medium`), briefed as the end of `references/drafting.md` says for a section writer, with the to-do rows in place of a capability and the room's estimate for each row as its budget. Its brief has a `Files:` line (the to-do prints one for each section; run `lint_brief.py` on the brief). It writes only the new and changed lines in `cov-A<n>.md` (heading `## A<n> <title>`; an edited line keeps its id), through Stages 0 to 3 of drafting.md, with its own `work/` files: `work/choice.md` holds the to-do rows it will write and, for each file under `Files:`, the timeout, bad input and down-dependency line of Stage 0, because `check_citations.py --audit` wants it. Then it puts each line in the section file at the place the to-do names, and runs `stamp.py` on the section file. It adds nothing the to-do does not name.
- **The condition audit.** A coverage line is where a value gets stated without the guard, branch or exception that limits it. Every line a writer adds or edits, and every Part 1 sentence a coverage step adds, is audited as the main writers' lines are (Stage 3 of drafting.md), row by row, before it goes into the guide:
  - The writer opens the cited line and the whole function around it, lists its early exits, flags, branches and other callers (`check_citations.py --sheet`, `--guards`, and the second, harsher pass), and each one is either stated in the line or the line is narrowed to the case it read. A fact that needs more conditions than a sentence of 18 words can carry is split, each part keeping its condition, or narrowed.
  - For an edited line the audit row covers the whole line: its old conditions are checked again, not only what the edit adds, and the edit may not drop one.
  - `python3 -I <skill>/scripts/check_citations.py cov-A<n>.md <repo> --max-words <rows x estimate> --max-sentence-words 18 --audit work/audit.md` must exit 0, with `work/choice.md`, `work/guards.md` (from `--guards`) and `work/evidence.md` beside the audit file: the same check the main writers pass. It audits `cov-A<n>.md` only, never the whole section file.
  - The front writer of the Part 1 additions writes `work/part1-audit.md` by hand, one row for each sentence it adds: `<its tag> | the tagged line says: <value, conditions, labels> | the sentence says: <the same, as worded> | result: HOLDS / narrowed to "..." / dropped`. Every condition and label the tagged line states is in the sentence, or the sentence is narrowed, or dropped.
- **Part 1.** After the appendix lines are in (`assemble.py <work> <repo> --index` lists their ids), one fresh front writer (`Seat: lead (skill writer), Opus 5.5 medium`), briefed as the front writer of drafting.md, adds for each core fact of the to-do one plain sentence of at most 18 words to the right section of `part1.md`, tagged to its appendix line (the new line, or the edited one), while Part 1 has room (the to-do says how many words are left). A fact that does not fit stays in the appendix only. The to-do's core rows are the only ones that get a Part 1 sentence.
- **Guard.** `assemble.py <work> <repo> -o GUIDE.md --clean`, then `lint_main.py`. A coverage sentence that pushes Part 1 over 1,000 words or fails a lint check is fixed once by the front writer, then dropped: its fact stays in the appendix, and Part 1 keeps only the core facts it can say in plain words. Do not shorten the other Part 1 sentences to make room. The reader check (step 14) still gates.

Add no security, privacy or threat language: the lister, the writers and the grader are told so in their briefs.

## Repair (steps 12 to 15)

Step 12 grades the draft that survived the fix rounds (label `repair`, the same `facts.txt`, a fresh grader) and works the to-do as above. Step 13 then checks the whole guide, the lines step 12 added included. The target is 98% of facts COVERED, and 98% of checked claims HOLDS. Step 16 grades again (label `final`) after the last cut and adds nothing: its figures are the ones the report quotes. A final below 98% is reported with its to-do file; the caller decides on another pass.

## The full check (step 13)

Cut the whole guide into slices as step 10 does, checkers with `references/check-brief.md`, reports in `reports-full`, scored against `slices-full`. Keep the copy of the guide the checkers saw as `GUIDE.full.cut.md`. Fix each finding in the section files and `part1.md` (not in `GUIDE.md`, which `assemble.py` writes again) by the repair rule in `references/fix-rounds.md`, then run `assemble.py`, `check_citations.py` and `lint_main.py`. Nothing re-checks those fixes until step 15.
